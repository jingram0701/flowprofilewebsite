Standing seam vs profiled steel cladding: which suits your home?

By Brenton J Fitzgerald, Licensed Plumber · 15 October 2026 · 2 min read

Steel cladding comes in two main styles. Both are durable and low maintenance. The difference is mostly in the look, the cost and the installation.

Standing seam

Standing seam panels join with a raised, concealed seam, so there are no visible fixings on the face. The result is long, clean vertical lines and a modern, architectural finish.

  • Clean look with no exposed screws
  • Suits modern homes, extensions and feature walls
  • Takes more time and skill to install, so it costs more

Profiled sheeting

Profiled cladding uses corrugated or ribbed sheets fixed through the face. It is the classic Australian look, and it is quicker to install.

  • Cost-effective and quick to install
  • Suits sheds, garages, rural and traditional homes
  • Fixings are visible, so neat, straight lines matter

Which one?

If the cladding is the feature of the house, standing seam is usually worth it. For a garage, shed or a large wall where budget matters, profiled sheeting does the job well. Many homes use both: standing seam on the front and profiled sheeting where it is less visible.

Whichever you choose

The finish comes down to the details: corners, window and door surrounds, and where the wall meets the roof. That is where water gets in and where a job looks sharp or sloppy.
