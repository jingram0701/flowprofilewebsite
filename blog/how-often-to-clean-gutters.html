How often should you clean your gutters in Melbourne?

By Brenton J Fitzgerald, Licensed Plumber · 15 October 2026 · 2 min read

The short answer is at least once a year, and twice if you have trees nearby. The best answer depends on what is growing around your house.

A simple rule of thumb

  • No trees close by: once a year, ideally in late autumn before winter rain.
  • Some trees nearby: twice a year, in autumn and again in spring.
  • Large gums or overhanging trees: every three months, as gum leaves and bark build up quickly.

Signs your gutters need attention

  • Water spilling over the edge during rain
  • Plants or grass growing in the gutter
  • Stains on the fascia or walls below
  • Gutters sagging or pulling away from the fascia
  • Damp patches on eaves or ceilings

What happens if you leave them

Blocked gutters overflow. Water then runs behind the gutter into the fascia and eaves, where it rots timber, or down the walls to the footings. Leaves sitting wet in steel gutters also speed up rust. A blocked gutter is cheap to clear and expensive to ignore.

When cleaning is not enough

If your gutters still overflow when they are clear, the problem is usually size or fall. Gutters that are too small for the roof area, or set without enough slope to the downpipe, will overflow in heavy rain no matter how clean they are. That is a job for replacement, not a clean.
